Red lentils: India's demand window and what it means for Saskatchewan

A quietly extended duty-free window in Delhi is doing more for prairie growers than any domestic policy this year. Here's how the trade is repositioning.

5 May 2026 By the Mercatorum desk 6-minute read

India is the single biggest variable in the red-lentil market and has been for two decades. When New Delhi opens the import window, prices rise in Saskatchewan; when it closes, growers carry the inventory. The country recently extended its duty-free import window by another six months — quietly, without much press coverage — and the trade is adjusting.

What the policy actually does

The duty-free window applies to masoor (red lentils) and a handful of other pulses. Without it, importers face a 30%+ basic customs duty that effectively prices Canadian and Australian product out of the market. With it, Indian buyers can step in at the marginal cost of freight and trade finance.

The extension came at a moment when Indian domestic production was softer than expected. Combine those two facts and you get a buying window that's structurally bullish for prairie red lentils into Q3.

"India is the biggest variable in the lentil market and always has been. What changes is whether the door is open or closed."

Canada and Australia compete head-to-head into the same dominant buyer. The relevant question for growers in Estevan or Swift Current is what Australia is doing — and Australian product is moving early and quickly, which gives Canadian sellers room to firm CFR offers later in the season.

Where farm-gate prices likely settle

  1. Near term (May–June). Cash bids at the elevators hold firm. Container freight remains the main wildcard. Watch the Vancouver–Mumbai container rate weekly.
  2. Mid term (July–September). If India's window holds and Australian product clears, we'd expect another CAD $20–40/t lift on top-grade No. 2 red lentils, dehulled.
  3. Tail risk. India's policy can reverse on six weeks' notice. Growers carrying inventory deep into Q4 are taking that risk explicitly.

What we're doing

For producers we work with, we're pricing on staggered windows — not on hope. For importers, we're quoting CFR Mumbai and CFR Chittagong on rolling 60-day terms and recommending phytosanitary booking earlier than usual; the inspection backlog at Vancouver has been longer than typical this spring.
