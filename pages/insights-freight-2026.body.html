Dry-bulk freight: why the Pacific keeps surprising the curve

Panamax rates have refused to settle into the consensus range for two quarters running. We think it's structural — and it changes the math on CFR vs FOB for grain out of Vancouver.

28 Apr 2026 By the Mercatorum desk 5-minute read

Every quarter, the freight curve tells you what the market thinks will happen. And for two quarters now, the Pacific has refused to oblige. Panamax day-rates have settled meaningfully above where the FFA market priced them three months earlier. That's not noise; that's information.

What the curve was saying — and what happened

QuarterFFA forward (3M prior)Actual TC averageSurprise
Q4 2025$11,400/day$13,900/day+$2,500
Q1 2026$10,200/day$12,600/day+$2,400

A consistent under-pricing of forward rates by ~$2,500/day across two quarters isn't a market that's mispricing weather — it's a market that hasn't fully priced in structural changes on the supply side of shipping.

What we think is structural

  1. Slow-steaming under IMO rules. The EEXI and CII rules continue to bite. Effective fleet capacity is lower than headline DWT suggests.
  2. Yard backlog. Newbuild deliveries for 2026 are below the long-run average. The order book skewed to container and tanker tonnage during the 2022 cycle, not bulkers.
  3. Asia coal flows. Indonesian and Australian coal demand into China and India is running ahead of expectation. That ties up Panamax tonnage that would otherwise rotate into grain trades.
"The market is pricing freight like 2019. The fleet is operating like 2026. Those two facts have to converge."

What it means for grain out of Vancouver

The simplest implication: CFR offers will run firmer than the FOB curve suggests. Importers buying grain CFR into Northeast Asia have been getting numbers that already build in higher physical freight than the paper market shows. Sellers willing to take freight risk and quote FOB have been winning marginal trades.

For a Canadian exporter, two practical takeaways:

  • Don't price internal logistics + ocean freight off the FFA curve. Use a +$2,500/day overlay for at least the next two quarters.
  • Where your buyer is willing, quote FOB and let them carry the freight. Right now they probably can't beat your number anyway.

What we'd be wrong about

A China slowdown that knocks 50M tonnes off seaborne coal demand would do the work. So would a faster-than-expected yard ramp on bulker newbuilds. Neither is our base case for the next four quarters, but both belong on a freight-trader's wall.
