01 · Incoterms 2020

Who carries the risk, who pays the freight.

Incoterms 2020 are the eleven standard rules the International Chamber of Commerce maintains for how cost and risk pass from seller to buyer. Here are the five we use most in bulk trade.

FOB
Free On Board

Seller delivers the goods onto the ship at the named port of loading. Cost and risk pass when the cargo is on board.

Use forBulk vessels, sea/inland waterway
Export clearanceSeller
Ocean freightBuyer
Marine insuranceBuyer
Risk transferOn board vessel
CFR
Cost and Freight

Seller pays ocean freight to the named destination port. Risk still passes at the loading port, so the buyer carries the voyage risk.

Use forBulk vessels, sea only
Export clearanceSeller
Ocean freightSeller
Marine insuranceBuyer
Risk transferOn board at load port
CIF
Cost, Insurance & Freight

Same as CFR, plus the seller buys marine insurance for the voyage on the buyer's behalf (typically minimum cover under ICC C).

Use forBulk vessels, sea only
Export clearanceSeller
Ocean freightSeller
Marine insuranceSeller
Risk transferOn board at load port
FCA
Free Carrier

Seller delivers to a carrier nominated by the buyer at the named place. Common for containerised cargo where the cargo doesn't go directly onto a vessel.

Use forContainers, any mode
Export clearanceSeller
Main carriageBuyer
InsuranceBuyer
Risk transferCargo to nominated carrier
DAP
Delivered At Place

Seller delivers — and bears all risk — to the named destination place. Buyer handles import clearance and duties. The most seller-heavy term we typically use.

Use forAny mode, including overland to inland buyer
Export clearanceSeller
Main carriageSeller
Import clearance & dutiesBuyer
Risk transferAt delivery point, ready for unloading

02 · Document checklists

Paperwork that holds up at any port.

What we routinely produce or arrange for each commodity class. Your buyer's L/C terms may add to this list — these are the standards.

Agriculture / Grain

Grain & pulses

  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list / weight note
  • Clean on-board bill of lading
  • Certificate of origin (Canada — CETA/CUSMA/CPTPP forms)
  • Phytosanitary certificate (CFIA)
  • Weight & quality certificate (independent surveyor — SGS, Intertek, etc.)
  • Fumigation certificate (if buyer requires)
  • Insurance certificate (CIF only)

Energy

Crude oil & refined products

  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of lading (3 originals)
  • Certificate of origin
  • Certificate of quality (independent inspector)
  • Certificate of quantity (shore tank or ullage report)
  • Certificate of insurance (CIF only)
  • Master's receipt for samples
  • Cargo manifest
  • Letter of indemnity (if BLs delayed)

Chemicals

Industrial chemicals & fertilizer

  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of lading
  • Certificate of origin
  • Safety data sheet (SDS / MSDS)
  • Certificate of analysis (CoA)
  • Dangerous-goods declaration (if applicable)
  • IMO Class & UN number (DG cargo)
  • Insurance certificate (CIF only)

04 · Glossary

Trade terms, explained briefly.

The terms that come up in almost every cargo. Short definitions — enough to follow the conversation.

Basis
The difference between a local cash price and the related futures price. Tells you what physical buyers are paying right now relative to the paper market.
Bill of lading (B/L)
The carrier's receipt for the cargo. Functions as a document of title — whoever holds the original BL controls the cargo.
Carry
The cost of holding a physical commodity over time — storage, financing, insurance. Reflected in the futures curve when later months trade higher than nearer ones.
Charter party
The contract between a shipowner and a charterer to use a vessel. Voyage charter (one trip) or time charter (a defined period).
Demurrage
A penalty paid by the charterer to the shipowner when loading or discharging takes longer than the agreed laytime.
FFA
Forward Freight Agreement. A paper hedge on dry-bulk freight, used to lock in transport costs.
Hedge
An offsetting position taken to reduce the risk of an existing physical position — usually in a related futures or paper market.
L/C
Letter of credit. A bank's commitment to pay the seller when the seller presents the agreed shipping documents.
Phytosanitary
A government-issued certificate confirming a plant-based cargo is free of regulated pests. Issued in Canada by the CFIA.
Spread
The price difference between two related contracts — different months, different commodities, or different delivery locations.
Surveyor
Independent third party that measures weight, samples quality, and issues certificates used to settle the trade. SGS, Intertek, Bureau Veritas are common names.
Tender
A formal procurement process — usually government or large parastatal — inviting offers under defined terms. Common in fertilizer and food-aid trade.
